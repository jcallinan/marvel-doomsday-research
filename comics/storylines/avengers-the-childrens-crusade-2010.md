# *Avengers: The Children's Crusade* (2010–2012) — Doom, Wanda and the Young Avengers

**Continuity:** Marvel Comics, not MCU canon. **Research updated:** November 11, 2026, prompted by a new *VisionQuest* reveal, **not a newly released 2026 comic**. **Creative team:** Allan Heinberg (writer), Jim Cheung (principal artist), per [Marvel's series catalog](https://www.marvel.com/comics/series/9864/avengers%3A_the_children%27s_crusade_%282010_-_2012%29). Core miniseries: **#1–9** published 2010–2012. The [2012 collected edition](https://www.marvel.com/comics/collection/31727/avengers_the_childrens_crusade_hc_hardcover) also includes the *Young Avengers* tie-in.

## Verified comics narrative (publisher summaries, paraphrased)

1. **The search:** Billy Kaplan/Wiccan's increasingly potent reality manipulation prompts the Young Avengers to look for his missing mother, Scarlet Witch. See [*The Children's Crusade* #1, published July 8, 2010](https://www.marvel.com/comics/issue/31134/avengers_the_childrens_crusade_2010_1) and [Marvel's collected-edition overview](https://www.marvel.com/comics/collection/31727/avengers_the_childrens_crusade_hc_hardcover).
2. **Wanda, Doom and Latveria:** [Marvel's official Young Avengers background](https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/young-avengers) says the search reaches **Latveria**, where Wanda is found without her usual memories and powers and betrothed to Doom; he captures Wiccan, creating a conflict among the teams seeking her.
3. **Doom desires Wanda's power:** [*The Children's Crusade* #3, published November 10, 2010](https://www.marvel.com/comics/issue/31138/avengers-the-childrens-crusade-2010-3) foreshadows a villain's desire to possess Wanda. [*The Children's Crusade* #5, published April 6, 2011](https://www.marvel.com/comics/issue/31142/read), places Doom and Magneto in conflict in Latveria while Wanda's reality-changing magic impacts the timestream. [Marvel's Young Avengers history](https://www.marvel.com/teams-and-groups/young-avengers) explicitly describes Doom later claiming Wanda's reality-altering power and battling the Young Avengers, Avengers and X-Men.
4. **Consequences and divergence:** Comic plot events include a confrontation involving Stature, Iron Lad, and an altered past, which **must not** be imported into the MCU without independent confirmation. The MCU already has its own distinct storyline for Vision and the twins.

## Why this comic matters in November 2026

- **Corroborated MCU scene:** The Disney+ *VisionQuest* finale includes a post-credits scene where Doom finds Wanda after Mount Wundagore and makes clear he wants her power. It follows a Vision/Billy/Tommy reunion. [EW reported this on Nov. 10 at 11:01 p.m. ET](https://ew.com/visionquest-avengers-doomsday-post-credits-scene-elizabeth-olsen-scarlet-witch-return-12248438); [Disney+'s official schedule names Nov. 11 as the finale date](https://www.disneyplus.com/explore/articles/marvel-visionquest). See [Nov. 11 research ledger](../../news/2026-11-11.md).
- **Strong *parallel*, not confirmation of literal adaptation:** Both narratives involve a missing Wanda, her children, Doom's interest, and coveted reality-altering power. The MCU scene says **nothing definitive** about comic-book betrothal, memory loss, how Doom acquires power, an identical resolution, or Wanda's appearance in the *Doomsday* film's official cast list.
- **Separate cosmology:** Hickman's 2015 *Secret Wars* uses Battleworld and God Emperor Doom; *Children's Crusade* is a **different work**. Do not conflate the two even when building a composite film hypothesis.
